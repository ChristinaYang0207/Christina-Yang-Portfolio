Cracking the Happiness Code by Numbers

Can happiness be defined by data?

Background

Happiness is a concept that has been floating around for centuries. However, what exactly is happiness? What makes us have such an enormous amount of joy and contentment? Unsurprisingly, any professional would spend their lifetime trying to find an accurate answer for this important question. Endless theories would be published. Some ideas were created through pure subjectiveness; while others did endless amount of research as evidence. In reality, it is hard to say which one is right and wrong.

In this project, I want to objectively view happiness by using data to see external factors that brings the most happiness to people. Specifically, I am interested in seeing how happiness is connected to economics, health, support and corruption. The data visualization and observations can open up perspectives about different countries, as well as supporting or defying existing theories. I decide to select datasets from the year 2020, as it was the start of COVID-19 pandemic that brought extra hardship to many individuals

We're Analyzing Happiness by:

Economics

Study of production, consumption, and transfer of wealth.

Health

The life expectancy of the country, as well as checking out the latest issue of COVID-19 towards the end.

Corruption

People's perception on unethical practices and unfair treatments in the field of business and politics.

Social Support

Having a stable network, including getting help from friends, relatives, and communities.

Data Visualization

Using the datasets, I created infographics to analyze happiness level by seeing the correlation between different variables.

Findings

Developing countries are placed in the least ladder scores, while the developed countries with the highest ladder scores

The highest 10 countries are all developed countries, while the last then are all developing countries.

Regions consisted of many developing countries tend to have a more variety of ladder score, while regions consisted of many developed countries tend to have less

North America and ANZ seems to have the smallest variation between the countries in the same region when it comes to ladder score. On the other hand, Middle East and North Africa tied with South Asia for the most variation.

Ladder score has a positive correlation with GDP per capita, life expectancy, and social support

The result can support the Maslow's motivation triangle, which outlines the level of human needs that are required to fulfil for an individual's self-growth and well-being.

Developing countries high % of loss when it comes to HDI vs IHDI

Western Europe Regions such as Norway have generally high HDI in 2020 due to high cooperation between people as a whole, positive environment and network for success. On the other hand, Central African Republic have one of the lowest HDIl Factors may include the lack of necessities, inflation, inequality, and lack of contribution to societal development.

Ladder score has a negative correlation with perception of corruption

Having negative slope indicates that when people believe that there is high corruption in business or government, their overall state is dissatisfaction and unjoyful. Common form of corruption may include nepotism and bribery.

Many of the developing country shown to be generous when donating, despite their low income

There isn't a defined reason as the factors highly depends on culture, institution, experience, situation, psychology, and neurology. However, an article from the University of Notre Dame published by Christian Smith suggests "natural selection would have worked against human ancestors who did not practice the giving, sharing, and sometimes self-sacrifice necessary for the collective survival of one’s immediate group".

Citizens of most country view their own country as corruptive but have some social support

Based on the data, most citizens view their country as corruptive but have friends or families to get support. The lack of reliance may be due to the disagreement with the justice system and lack of regulations. However, the correlations between the two are weak.

Covid-19 affected most of the fields, particular economics, health and corruption.

During COVID-19, many businesses, especially smaller ones, suffered from lack of sales and employees. The government constantly trying to respond by making new policies and send support. The rich, although affecting everyone, seems to have an advantage during the pandemic.
