Giant Eagle Item Portal

How can we enhance the item portal to streamline the digitalization process and simplify data uploads for vendors?

Background

Giant Eagle, headquartered in Cranberry, Pennsylvania, operates approximately 400 stores across multiple formats in states including Pennsylvania, Ohio, West Virginia, Maryland, and Indiana. An opportunity was given to me by Break Through Tech, who collaborated with Giant Eagle to allow students to complete a micro-internship at the respective company.

Within a three-week sprint, my team worked together to help propose an item portal design and present our prototype to other team leaders in Giant Eagle. My primary responsibilities are completing the wireframes and phototypes of the application.

Role

  • My role: Lead Designer
  • Teammates: Me (Lead Designer), Morgan Rapsky (Project Manager), Ifeoluwa Olusoga (Developer), Adetayo Adenekan (Developer), Irwin Jiang (Developer)
  • Sector: Retail, Supply Chain
  • Tools: Lucid, Figma, Jira, Github, JSCode, Canva, Powerpoint
  • Time: 3 weeks

Problem Statement

The problem that we were presented with is that‍

Giant Eagle doesn't have a way to efficiently keep track of their inventories that are coming in and out.‍

‍Keeping a clean and accurate data is very important for business development in general and this project is a big focus within the company right now as they're trying to develop AI tools utilizing these data. Hence, our objective is to conceptualize an item portal that allows vendors to upload new item, add replacement item, and quickly edit any errors.
‍
What we discovered the issues with the current item system (excel and paper/pencil) are that:

1

Time Intensive

Vender had to repeatedly refill every field out due to the lack of automation for new items, causing each sheet to take 20-25 minutes to complete.

2

Data Accuracy Issues 

Sheets often have common errors such as include UPC codes, case packs, vendor numbers, costs, case dimensions, item descriptions, abbreviations.

3

Delay Procurement 

The time intensive process makes it hard for product to get onto the shelf on time. Speed is what gives Giant Eagle the competitive edge.

So how can we enhance the item portal to streamline the digitalization process and simplify data uploads for vendors?‍

To address the issue of the current item system, here is our proposed solution:

01

Verification

Attributes must be properly filled out, changes on forms must be approved by Data Integrity Team before changes are made

02

Less Manual Entry

Make sure to have dropdowns, pre-existing categories, autofill for replacement products

03

Condensed Forms

Select from a limited number of forms, forms change based on need, etc., information saved for replacement forms

04

Hierarchical Format

Allow for white space, creating drop-down, grouping together like information under a common header

05

Field Requirement

More specific description requirements to combat item description inaccuracies, error messages

06

Easy Correction

If mistakes are made on the sheet, give vendor a way to easy correct the error and resubmit it for approval.

Approaching an Unknown Subject

Flowchart

The biggest obstacle for us is understanding the terminology and functionality of a typical item portal. To help us understand how vendors would interact with the product, we created several use cases to highlight potential scenarios of why the item portal is used, who it is used by, and functionality of what we should be focused on.

The flowchart is created through lucid to allow our client to see how the item portal should theoretically work from start to finish. The step also helped us determine the scale of the project and how it could fit in the Giant Eagle model. We also had experienced members from other teams to give us feedback and letting us know if we are in the right track.

Competitive Analysis

Competitive analysis allows us to know existing item portal in the market. Walmart is a direct competition that we were able to have access to through videos:

Likes: Modernized look within a one-in-all portal, able to save what you uploaded and edit it for later, and sufficient product details shown just by the dashboard.

Dislikes: Too many steps to just upload the product, overwhelming number of options that can stress new users, didn’t include what type of distribution method an item is.

We separated pages and functions into many different sections so it would be easier to understand. A clearer image of the prototypes is shown in the  high-fidelity subsection.

Wireframes

After the flowchart, we started making the wireframes. This step is mainly for sketching out different design ideas - both traditionally and digitally - while having the conversation regarding the functionality of the item portal. We compiled them into the PowerPoint as it was the most accessible platform for all of us, and everyone is able to edit.

Low-Fidelity Prototype

After determining what should go into the design, we transferred our ideas from PowerPoint to Figma. As the sole designer on the team, I handled all of the work for the low-fidelity and high-fidelity prototypes. Although low-fidelity looks similar to wireframes, there is a stronger emphasis on design consistency and the overall navigation from pages to pages. It also helped a lot in determining what components that need to be used and made for our final product.

Some examples of our low-fidelity prototypes.

High-Fidelity Prototype

Once the low-fidelity was completed, we started the high-fidelity. This is primarily used for the development process and would be the closest look to what we want the final product to look like. We used the design system, but we also made our own components. We also made a video demo for the presentation to demonstrate how the final product would function.

Account Login

Vendors can use their Giant Eagle emails to login to their platforms.

Edit Profile

Vendors can update their account by clicking on account setting, helping them customize the portal to their likings!

Submit Forms

Vendors will select the type of form, fill out the information, and submit it to be reviewed. The ideas are that the form have to be similar to their current form, but more accessible and readable. To also prevent any errors, validation and field requirement system is in place.  

Item Status Page

Vendors to check their submitted form process, see submitted/rejected forms, and go back to saved forms.

Resubmitting Forms

If a form is denied, the vendor can reedit and resubmit the form. The system will indicate what field contains errors.

Receipt

When the form is successfully sent to the team to be submitted or is already accepted, vendors are able to click on 'view form from the Item Status to see what they have wrote.

Feedback

Something that I want to highlight is the importance of feedback. We received great suggestions throughout the whole project, but it was especially important during the design as it is a closer look of our final product. Here are some examples of how we take feedback to help iterate our design: 

"How does the vendor know if a request is in-progress?"

Instead of just a deny and approve status, we added in-progress and incomplete status to make it clearer if a form is submitted

"How can we eliminate errors throughout the form before sending it off to be approved?"

The idea was originally in our flowchart but isn’t properly shown in the prototype, hence we added a few frames to demonstrate validation errors being checked before submission and errors that may cause the form to be denied.

"Giant Eagle layout and consistency to follow the brand?"

We requested access to the design system and use it into our own project while creating components that following the structures.

Develop and Deliver

Development

After completing the design, the prototype is handed over to the programmers to complete the front-end development. They were able to use the Figma Dev Mode to get accurate CSS configuration of the components. Due to the lack of time, the product is not entirely functional. However, our goal is to demonstrate how the product would work theoretically on a technical device. For instance, our login screen would show error would be deal when typing incorrect password and username.

Presentation

After making a slide deck through Canva, the project is presented to approximately 20 Giant Eagle team members. The response is overall positive, and they were impressed by the work done within 3 weeks. This wrapped up our three weeks work with the company.

What I learned

I was not informed where the project is going. The micro-internship has given me a lot of experience. Here are some things I have learned:

Working at a Corporate Setting

For the first two weeks, we had the opportunity to talk to members of Giant Eagle about their day-to-day life. Many business terminologies have also been thrown around, including high level, agile, and sprint. Standups and regular product checkups were also been new practices to me.  

Importance of Product Management

To make the most out of the project, we used Microsoft Teams and Jira to plan out individual tasks and frequently check in with each other. Our success allowed us to not only created the bare minimum deliverable of wireframes, but also have a semi-functional product demonstration using website development codes.

Understanding the supply chain

For this particular project, supply chain concepts have been very heavy. Due to the internal nature, none of the members have any expertise in the area. In addition to the help of the team leaders, we done a lot of research ourselves to grasp the whole problem.

Working with a team with different backgrounds

All of the team members have different field of expertise. For instance, our group consisted of students majoring in different subjects: two computer science, one health informatics, one policy studies & criminology, and one Digital Narrative and Interactive Design (me), We were able to split our work based on our skills and made the most out of time.
